Abstract

Automated demonstration synthesis for imitation learning avoids costly data collection, but transferring demonstrations across morphologies often produces trajectories that the target robot cannot execute or that collide with obstacles. Mobile manipulation amplifies these challenges as the planner must coordinate base placement and arm motion simultaneously. We present CroMoGen, a learning-free, planning-based framework that synthesizes mobile manipulation demonstrations from different robot and human sources. CroMoGen formulates synthesis as a constrained optimization problem that combines kinematic feasibility, whole-body collision avoidance, and a trajectory alignment objective that preserves task-relevant object interactions. The approach adapts demonstrated motions to the target morphology and environment. Further, unlike methods that decouple base placement from arm motion, CroMoGen jointly optimizes both for greater adherence to the source demonstration. We evaluate CroMoGen on four mobile manipulation task families in RoboCasa and ParaHome. In the challenging human-to-robot setting, CroMoGen reaches a 56% demonstration-generation success rate, exceeding the strongest baseline by 37 percentage points.

Method Overview

CroMoGen is a planning method for building demonstration datasets. It retargets demonstrations recorded from humans or other robots to a target mobile manipulator. CroMoGen treats each demonstration as a description of an interaction: which object moves, along which path, and when contact begins and ends. It follows this interaction closely. Outside contact, it follows the path of the demonstrator's hand or end effector only loosely, and it does not reproduce the motion of the demonstrator's limbs or joints. For this reason, it needs no paired source–target demonstrations, no joint-level correspondence and no learned retargeting policy. CroMoGen retargets each demonstration in four stages, numbered by figure panel below.

  1. Reference construction (panels 1–4). CroMoGen divides the demonstration into interaction phases and builds a reference path for the target robot's gripper. It replaces the demonstrator's grasp with one generated for the target gripper with GraspGen. During contact, the gripper stays fixed relative to the object and follows the object's demonstrated motion. Before and after contact, the demonstrated hand path is bent to end at the new grasp, and its shape is kept.
  2. Coupled base–arm search (panels 5–6). At a small set of planning frames, CroMoGen samples candidate base poses on a grid and computes arm configurations for each with inverse kinematics. Dynamic programming then chooses one candidate per frame. This sets the base and the arm together, and it keeps the gripper close to the reference while limiting travel time. Consecutive base poses must be reachable in the time that the demonstration allows.
  3. Collision verification and repair (panel 7). CroMoGen checks the chosen solution for collisions, repairs or removes the colliding candidates, and runs the search again.
  4. Execution timing and simulated evaluation (panel 8). CroMoGen sets the timing of the trajectory within the robot's speed limits and runs it in physics simulation. It keeps the demonstration only if the task succeeds.

Tasks, Robots and Sources

Human-to-robot retargeting uses 529 human demonstrations from the ParaHome motion-capture dataset. Each demonstration is a segment of a longer recording, and it covers one of four household tasks: pick_cup (164 segments), move_cup (164), push_chair (40) and close_refrigerator (161). Each segment is retargeted to all three robots.

Robot-to-robot retargeting is evaluated in two settings. In ParaHome, the sources are the demonstrations that CroMoGen generated for the three robots in the human-to-robot setting, and each one is retargeted to every robot. In RoboCasa, the sources are the teleoperated Panda-Omron demonstrations provided with RoboCasa for ten atomic tasks. Four are pick-and-place tasks: PickPlaceCounterToMicrowave, PickPlaceMicrowaveToCounter, PickPlaceCounterToOven and PickPlaceDrawerToCounter. Six open or close a fridge, dishwasher, cabinet or drawer: OpenFridge, CloseFridge, OpenDishwasher, CloseDishwasher, OpenCabinet and CloseDrawer. CroMoGen retargets them to Spot, to Unitree G1 and to Panda-Omron itself.

The robots have different bodies. Spot has legs and one arm. Panda-Omron has a wheeled base and a 7-DoF arm with a torso lift. Unitree G1 is a humanoid.
